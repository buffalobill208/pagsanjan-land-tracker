## Market Interpretation

The asking-price freeze now reaches eleven consecutive weeks. No new listings have entered any monitored category, no existing listings have reduced their price, and no listings have disappeared since the September 14 delisting of Pagsanjan Barangay I (13,000 sqm, PHP 700/sqm). Whether that delisting represented a completed sale or an expired listing remains unconfirmed — if it was a sale, it is the most recent confirmed Pagsanjan-core transaction, priced at a substantial discount to current ask levels.

The persistent inversion — Pagsanjan-core median (PHP 1,300/sqm) sitting below the ring (PHP 2,000/sqm) — remains a composition effect: the Pagsanjan core is dominated by two large parcels (5+ ha) priced at PHP 850–1,100/sqm, while smaller ring parcels asking PHP 2,000–2,800/sqm skew the ring median upward. This is not evidence that Pagsanjan-core land is cheaper per comparable parcel.

**Pricing guidance for a raw 0.5–1 ha Pagsanjan parcel:**
- Competitive asking price: PHP 1,400–1,800/sqm
- Fastest-sale price: PHP 900–1,000/sqm

The only sub-1 ha Pagsanjan-core comparable in the dataset is Maulawin (4,767 sqm, PHP 2,000/sqm), which has been listed unchanged for eleven weeks and has not cleared — suggesting PHP 2,000/sqm may be above the market-clearing level for that size and location. The PHP 900–1,000/sqm fastest-sale recommendation is anchored on the September 14 clearance at PHP 700/sqm (Barangay I), adjusted upward for uncertainty about whether it sold or expired.

Aging-versus-cleared inventory remains unanswerable: no source publishes days-on-market and the only confirmed delisting this quarter has an ambiguous outcome.
