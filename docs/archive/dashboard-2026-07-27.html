Sourcing & honesty notes

  • Two confidence tiers. VERIFIED means a Dot Property individual post that was opened and read: area, total price and ₱/sqm all appear on the same post and the arithmetic reconciles. INDICATIVE means the figures came from a search-result snippet only — the arithmetic reconciles, but the individual post could not be opened, so the link points to a search page, never a fabricated post URL. Treat indicative rows as directional context, not as a confirmed individual listing.
  • Dot Property allows automated fetching and supplied all 16 verified rows. Each listing here was opened individually — the summary pages alone were not trusted.
  • Lamudi blocks bots, so it was used for market context only and contributed no rows. It did independently corroborate four verified rows (the 51,328 / 29,434 / 4,767 / 10,454 sqm parcels), which is a useful cross-check. It surfaced one further Pagsanjan parcel — Dingin, 13,926 sqm at ₱1,250/sqm — that quotes no total price; it is not reconcilable and so was not admitted as a row.
  • OnePropertee is robots-disallowed and was read from search snippets only. It was the weak source this week. Its Pagsanjan snippets returned mutually contradictory figures for what appears to be a single 2,000 sqm listing — ₱600,000, ₱2.30M and ₱6.38M, with three different ₱/sqm claims. All were dropped rather than guessed at. Only one OnePropertee row survived reconciliation.
  • No listing dates exist. None of the three sources publishes a reliable post date, and no "updated X ago" stamp appeared on any row that survived verification. The table therefore cannot be sorted newest-first and defaults to ₱/sqm ascending. No date or days-on-market value has been invented.
  • Nearby-town pages are badly mis-tagged. Dot Property's Lumban and Majayjay land pages were largely filled with Nuvali, Sta Rosa, Carmona, Silang and Calauan listings far outside the ~10 km scope. Every nearby row above had its municipality confirmed from the post body rather than the page tag. Lumban contributed no usable rows; Pakil returned zero results.
  • What was deliberately excluded: parcels with significant structures (a farm house/barn/rice mill in Pila, an ancestral house in Sampaloc, a semi-finished house in Liliw); commercial-frontage pricing above ~₱15,000/sqm (three Pagsanjan lots at ₱16,000–₱28,000/sqm); parcels of 9–20 ha, far above the ~5 ha comparability ceiling; and rows whose stated ₱/sqm did not reconcile with area × price within 2% (including a Santa Cruz lot quoting ₱3,500/sqm that computes to ₱2,863).
  • Size caveat. Only three comparables in the whole set (Maulawin, Pag-Asa, Sumucab) fall inside the owner's 0.5–1 ha range. Per-sqm price runs inversely to parcel size here, so the medians — which are pulled down by 5 ha blocks — understate what a small parcel can ask. Every row carries its hectare figure beneath the area, and oversized parcels are flagged.

Market read

Verified asking prices run ₱269–₱3,000/sqm, median ₱1,200/sqm. Split by geography: Pagsanjan proper ₱500–₱3,000, median ₱975/sqm; nearby ring ₱269–₱3,000, median ₱1,600/sqm. The expected Pagsanjan premium does not appear — Pagsanjan's median sits ~40% below the ring's. That is composition, not discount: the ring sample skews to road-frontage and lake-view parcels at ₱2,000–₱3,000, while Pagsanjan contributes genuine back-lot farmland (Buboy ₱500, Anibong ₱547). The cheap floor is not Cavinti or Lumban as one might assume but the flat agricultural land west of the river — Mojon, Pila at ₱269/sqm and Calios, Santa Cruz at ₱450/sqm.

The set sorts into three bands: ₱269–₱700 bulk agricultural (large, interior, sold as farmland); ₱850–₱1,500 the working middle (titled, flat, road-accessible — the densest band, and where Pagsanjan inventory clusters); and ₱2,000–₱3,000 amenity and frontage (river/lake frontage, highway corners, or small parcels where the buyer pays for location). Critically, the sub-1-ha comparables — Maulawin 0.48 ha at ₱2,000, Pag-Asa 0.47 ha at ₱1,200, Sumucab 0.84 ha at ₱2,204 — sit in the top band. Per-sqm price runs inversely to parcel size, which is the most decision-relevant pattern in this data.

Aging vs cleared inventory cannot be assessed this week, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise: there is no prior snapshot, and no source publishes listing dates. Which ₱/sqm band sells fastest is genuinely unanswerable from one week of data — it needs the GONE signal, which arrives from next run onward.

Recommendation for a raw 0.5–1 ha Pagsanjan parcel: ask ₱1,600–₱1,800/sqm (₱8.0M–₱9.0M on 5,000 sqm; ₱16M–₱18M per hectare). The ₱975 Pagsanjan median is set by 5 ha blocks and is the wrong anchor for a small parcel; the right anchor is the sub-1-ha set at ₱1,200–₱2,204. Maulawin at ₱2,000/sqm is the closest true comparable — same municipality, same size class, fruit-bearing, road frontage — and it is still on the market, which argues against matching it. For quickest sale specifically, price at ₱1,400/sqm (₱7.0M on 5,000 sqm): just above the whole-market median, below every small-parcel comparable, and inside the ₱850–₱1,500 band where most inventory competes — visibly a value listing without signalling distress. Hold the ₱1,800 end if the parcel has river access or standing fruit trees; if it is interior with no frontage, start at ₱1,400 and treat ₱1,100 as the floor.
